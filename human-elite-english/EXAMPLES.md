# Calibration examples

These examples test voice, not factual accuracy. Verify real claims separately.

## Reply: dense correction voice

**Fails**

> One nuance: a ±6.1% implied move is a snapshot, so it changes with expiry and timing. Even if all eight closes landed inside the range, that doesn't prove an easy short-vol trade. Entry IV, strikes, spreads and exit timing still decide whether the trade worked.

Why it fails: it opens like a reviewer, stacks more specialist terms than a reply needs, and closes the conversation instead of joining it.

**Passes style TDD**

> That 8-for-8 stat is interesting. Which expiry and timestamp did you use for the 6.1% move? Landing inside the range still leaves the entry price and spread to check.

Why it works: it responds to the actual observation, asks for decision-changing detail, and keeps one limit in plain English.

## Reply: fake sophistication

**Fails**

> Worth noting, this paradigm shift unlocks a game-changing opportunity across the AI ecosystem.

Why it fails: nothing concrete supports the claim, and the language performs importance rather than showing it.

**Passes style TDD**

> The demand signal looks strong. I would still want to see who signed the contracts and how much capacity is already financed before treating it as durable revenue.

Why it works: the judgment is calm, the missing evidence is specific, and the limit changes the conclusion.

## Post opening

**Fails**

> In today's fast-paced market, investors need to look beyond the headlines. Let's unpack three key takeaways.

**Passes style TDD**

> AI data-center orders can rise long before the cash shows up. I track four clocks separately: orders, recognized revenue, operating profit, and free cash flow.

The second opening gives the claim and the analytical frame inside the visible timeline text.
