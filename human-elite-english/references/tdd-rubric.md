# Nine-gate style TDD

Run every gate after each meaningful revision. A draft is publish-ready only when all nine gates pass. Record one sentence of evidence for each gate. Do not average scores and do not offset a failure with strengths elsewhere.

## T1. Evidence boundary

**Pass:** Every real-world claim is supplied, sourced, clearly framed as inference, or explicitly marked unknown. Numbers have a date and scope when those details affect meaning.

**Fail:** The copy invents experience, quotes, precision, attribution, consensus, or certainty. A factual correction appears without publishable support.

## T2. Exact subject fit

**Pass:** A reader can identify the exact post, claim, company, event, or problem being answered. A reply engages the author's point in its opening sentence when possible.

**Fail:** The text could be pasted under many unrelated posts, merely repeats the source, or starts with a detached verdict.

## T3. One useful increment

**Pass:** Short copy contributes one primary increment: a fact, mechanism, distinction, implication, or decision-changing question. Longer copy gives each paragraph or thread post a new job.

**Fail:** The draft stacks caveats, synonyms, or unrelated proof points to appear comprehensive.

## T4. Human speaker position

**Pass:** The reader can tell why this person is speaking, what they know, and where their confidence ends. First person appears only when it carries a real judgment or uncertainty.

**Fail:** The voice is faceless, omniscient, falsely personal, or padded with generic praise and familiarity.

## T5. Conversation fit

**Pass:** The language acknowledges the existing conversation and leaves a natural place for response. A question is specific and answerable when the missing information matters.

**Fail:** The reply reads like a correction notice, abstract, lecture, press release, or engagement bait.

## T6. Elite judgment

**Pass:** The draft is selective, calm, and decision-relevant. It separates what matters from what is merely interesting, names the strongest limit, and avoids overstating the conclusion.

**Fail:** "Expertise" is performed through jargon, dominance, certainty, status, complexity, or vague sophistication.

## T7. Plain-English precision

**Pass:** Ordinary words carry the argument. Necessary specialist terms are explained by context or kept within the mode's jargon budget.

**Fail:** Noun stacks, abbreviations, abstract labels, or finance/research shorthand force the reader to decode the sentence.

## T8. Anti-template rhythm

**Pass:** Sentence lengths vary naturally; the opening and ending belong to this subject; transitions follow meaning. Recent copy does not reuse the same opener or correction pattern.

**Fail:** The draft uses model clichés, corporate language, a three-part rhetorical costume, repeated openers, or a forced takeaway/vision ending.

## T9. Platform and read-aloud fit

**Pass:** The copy fits the chosen format, its visible opening works on its own, and one read-aloud pass sounds like a professional peer speaking. Claims, links, attachments, and calls to action match the approved scope.

**Fail:** A reply is a mini paper, a long post hides its point after the fold, a thread is mechanically split, or the wording changes the approved action.

## Failure protocol

1. Mark the draft RED and name the failed gates.
2. Preserve the strongest live sentence or concrete material.
3. Revise the smallest span that caused the failure.
4. Rerun all nine gates and the deterministic script.
5. Set `style_tdd=PASS` only when every gate and the script pass. Warnings require human review but do not automatically fail unless the project says otherwise.
